**Problem Statement and Motivation**

Your project addresses the question: *"How will computer hardware prices change over the next 10 years (2024–2034)?"* The motivation is that hardware prices directly affect consumers, PC builders, and businesses making purchasing decisions. You cover 9 components: CPU, GPU, RAM, SSD, HDD, Motherboard, PSU, Monitor, and CPU Cooler. You also encoded real market events as motivation — COVID demand surge (2020), global chip shortage (2021), GPU/crypto peak (2021), and market correction (2022–2023).

**Data and Methodology**

Your data comes from real scraped datasets with these sizes:
- CPU: 180 products, GPU: 376, RAM: 667, SSD: 211, HDD: 136, Motherboard: 429, PSU: 160, Case: 449, CPU Cooler: 247

You then reconstructed a **72-month historical price index (Jan 2018 – Dec 2023)** calibrated to real medians. You applied **4 models**:

- **ARIMA(p,d,q)** — built from scratch using Ridge regression, auto-selects best order per category by grid searching p∈[2,4,6], d∈[0,1], q∈[0,2]

- **AR(p) + Seasonal** — uses 12, 24, 36, and 72-month sine/cosine cycle features detected by periodogram analysis
- **Adaptive Ensemble** — weights each model inversely proportional to its validation RMSE so the best model per category gets highest weight

**Discussion and Conclusion**

Key talking points from your results:

- **Storage gets cheaper** — SSD drops 43.9% and RAM drops 31.7%, consistent with Moore's Law and NAND flash price trends
- **GPU stays expensive** — only 2.2% drop over 10 years, driven by AI/gaming demand keeping prices elevated
- **PSU and CPU Cooler slightly rise** — due to increasing power demands from more powerful CPUs and GPUs
- **Dominant price cycles** detected by periodogram: 36-month semiconductor cycle and 72-month long supply/demand cycle appear in every category

**Limitations to mention:**
- Historical data is reconstructed/simulated from real medians, not true month-by-month historical prices
- No external factors modeled (tariffs, new technology breakthroughs, geopolitical events)
- ARIMA built from scratch without statsmodels may be less accurate than production implementations
- Confidence intervals widen significantly beyond 2028, meaning long-term predictions carry high uncertainty
